About Pharos

Pharos is a HackUTD 2026 prototype for disaster responders. Residents call a short automated hotline; Beacon turns consented reports into a changing urgency and area queue. No resident account, web form, or name is required.

One queue, human-owned decisions

  • Reported danger determines the suggested urgency. Suspicious, incomplete, and uncertain requests stay visible at that urgency with a separate human-review badge.
  • Context review highlights exact caller words supporting a concrete unsafe request or explicit false-report/resource-diversion concern. It cannot determine whether an emergency is real from speech alone.
  • Flagged, pending, or unavailable assessments require a responder to accept the current evidence before normal assignment or an ETA. Acceptance and dismissal need a recorded reason; dismissal preserves the request and history.
  • When recording consent was given, a reviewer can manually listen to private caller-only audio beside the transcript. Tone and pacing provide context, not an automatic lie score. Transcript-only intake remains available.
  • Fresh reports and conservative same-phone callbacks can update facts, urgency, area, history, and assignment review. Pharos reacts to new reports rather than continuously sensing floods.
  • A responder explicitly reports travel and an estimated arrival time. A claim alone never means help is on the way, and elapsed time never proves arrival.
Prototype evidence: One real consenting fictional Photon intake reached Beacon. A later hosted synthetic WebRTC check passed speech, saved facts, corrected location retention, completion, and cleanup. The responder-review and recording flow has its own automated and current-build phone acceptance; an earlier call or model check alone does not prove that entire flow.

Ordinary fear, anger, panic, urgent rescue requests, missing names, or corrected details are not fraud evidence. No supported concern is not a verified identity or truth guarantee. Humans own response decisions, and the corner guide explains loaded records rather than contacting emergency services.

Location is caller-reported and matched to configured prototype areas, not GPS or live flood sensing. Shared desk access and demonstration responder profiles do not verify operational identities. The urgency suggestions are not validated clinical triage or emergency dispatch.

Transcript and caller-audio sharing require consent. Audio begins only after permission is acknowledged; clear completed-call audio is discarded. Retained review recordings and transcript evidence expire within 24 hours of their source call and are removed on withdrawal. Recordings are encrypted and playable only by signed-in responders; provider processing has separate retention limits.

Phone contacts are encrypted, and caller-ID lookup is a cautious retrieval hint. Caller ID can be spoofed and never proves fraud or who is calling. A resident-facing warning app, push/SMS, and protected two-party calls are future work outside this responder-only demo.
