About Pharos

Pharos is a HackUTD 2026 prototype for safer disaster support. Flare is the resident phone path. Beacon is the responder website for a human-reviewed help queue and a separate suspicious-contact review. Residents do not need to fill out a website form.

What the demo shows

  • Partial help cases update as consented reports arrive. Returning reports can link to an earlier open situation after conservative same-situation and current-location checks, with timestamped history and reassessment when conditions change. A name is not required.
  • A queue that explains suggested urgency, keeps unknown locations visible with accountable follow-up, and records human priority changes with reasons. Responders can claim, resolve, and reopen cases; changed coverage calls for assignment review.
  • A fictional transcript workbench connected to a persistent backend, plus a Photon SIP → LiveKit voice-runtime implementation. The test number and intake routing resources exist; finishing the route and real speech recognition still require end-to-end testing.
  • Pressure Trace highlights exact wording and explains observed psychological pressure. A consenting test recipient can pair an optional private warning screen before a protected call and enable supported phone notifications. Help requests remain phone-only.
  • An animated corner guide that explains the records currently loaded in the response desk: queue counts, the selected case, coverage, and pressure warnings. Its grounded local answers do not call an external language model or emergency services.
Prototype status: The latest revision is awaiting deployment and uses fictional scenarios. The Photon phone route and an actual notification banner have not been verified. Pharos re-triages when new reports arrive; it does not continuously monitor a resident or water levels. Phone-number matching is a retrieval hint, not proof of identity. Urgency rules are not validated emergency triage, and humans own every response decision.

Calls must be deliberately routed through Pharos; it cannot inspect arbitrary calls to a personal number. Optional notifications require setup and permission. A provider accepting a warning does not prove it appeared on a phone or was read.

Transcript storage begins after clear consent. Pharos stores no raw audio; withdrawal stops collection and removes relevant evidence and private access. Transcript evidence and caller linkage expire within at most 24 hours, while minimal help-request facts may remain for follow-up. Call and speech providers also process the call.

The message checker looks only at wording. It does not analyze a caller’s voice, emotion, or identity. A warning means “verify this independently,” not “this is definitely a scam.”
