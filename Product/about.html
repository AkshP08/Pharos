About Pharos

Pharos is a HackUTD 2026 prototype for safer disaster support. Flare is the resident phone path. Beacon is the responder website for a human-reviewed help queue and a separate suspicious-contact review. Residents do not need to fill out a website form.

What Pharos does

  • Consented reports update partial help cases during calls. Returning reports can link to an earlier open situation after current-location and same-situation checks. Timestamped history shows changing conditions without requiring a name.
  • The desk explains suggested urgency, filters configured service areas, keeps unknown locations visible, and accounts for responder coverage and capacity. Humans can claim, own follow-up, review priority, resolve, and reopen cases.
  • The response desk can explicitly report that a responder is on the way and supply an arrival estimate. A claim alone does not promise help; estimates can expire or need review when the situation changes. Time passing never proves arrival.
  • Protected-call mode reviews the caller’s words for pressure toward payment, secret sharing, or device access. Warnings quote evidence and explain observed tactics. A paired optional warning app supports foreground display and permission-based phone notifications.
  • The animated corner guide explains the desk’s loaded records locally. It does not contact emergency services or invent incident information.
Verified prototype status: The responder backend is deployed, and one real consenting fictional Photon call reached speech recognition and saved a three-person case. The two-phone protected-call warning and actual notification banner still need a real device demonstration. The hosted worker reports Running, and a synthetic audio check reached speech recognition, persistence, and cleanup. That check also exposed a location-detail regression now being fixed, so current audio acceptance remains open. The old local worker is stopped; the operator must confirm the fixed version and selected mode before calling.

The single Photon number currently serves intake and protected-call test modes sequentially. Calls must be deliberately routed through Pharos; it cannot inspect arbitrary personal calls. The optional warning app is for pairing and warnings, not resident help intake. A provider accepting a warning does not prove it appeared or was read.

Current facts come from caller reports, not GPS or continuous flood sensing. Urgency rules are not validated emergency triage, demonstration profiles do not establish responder identity, and humans own real-world response decisions.

Phone contacts are encrypted at rest with separate help and prior-risk lookups. Three distinct previously flagged calls using a caller ID may trigger a cautious history notice. Caller ID can be spoofed; the notice does not prove who is calling, block a help request, or replace current speech review.

Transcript storage begins after clear consent. Pharos stores no raw audio. Withdrawal stops collection and scrubs relevant evidence and private access. Transcript evidence, contact/risk history, and private access expire within at most 24 hours; minimal structured request facts may remain. Call and speech providers also process the call under their own terms.

A warning means “pause the risky action and verify independently,” not “this person is definitely a scammer.” Context review can be wrong or unavailable; no warning is not a safety guarantee. It does not judge accent, voice tone, emotion, or identity, and should not delay immediate physical safety instructions.
