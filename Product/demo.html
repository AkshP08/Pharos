What you are watching

The recording shows the latest Overview, Transcript & audio, Review, Response, and History case layout, larger readable text, coverage profiles, theme controls, and a prepared training submission. Live clicks and backend transitions are recorded continuously; field-by-field typing is omitted.

The phone excerpt is real. The caller explicitly consented to publication of the short excerpt. Its completed status and generated transcript were verified in production. Only that approved saved record is staged in the private recording copy, so no unrelated callers appear. Training, review, assignment and ETA use fictional reports in that same current app; production requests remain unchanged.

Only the approved short audio excerpt is reused. Unrelated private call content is hidden, and credentials, private numbers and provider dashboards are excluded. The rest of the original call is omitted before the edit switches to its completed transcript.

Reported danger retains its urgency during verification. Human acceptance does not prove a report is true, assignment alone does not confirm travel, and an arrival estimate is not a guarantee.

Narration transcript

  1. Response desk. When someone needs help, a phone call should be enough. Pharos turns that call into a clear next step for responders.
  2. Real call excerpt. Here’s a short excerpt from a real call. [Original real call excerpt: Pharos asks for the situation and location; the caller reports Northbank Library South entrance, Zone 2.]
  3. Completed call transcript. That call is now complete. The team can read the generated transcript, check consent, and open the linked request. Nothing has to be copied by hand.
  4. Overview and priority. Overview brings the essentials together: where the caller is, what they need, and why the request is ranked this way. These are reported facts for people to review.
  5. Evidence and human review. For this fictional training report, the team needs to check a concern. The quoted words stay beside the request. Reported danger keeps its urgency while normal response actions wait for human review.
  6. Acceptance and assignment. The reviewer records a reason and accepts the report. Now an eligible responder can take ownership. That decision stays tied to the evidence, so everyone can see what was checked.
  7. Explicit response estimate. Here, the desk reports that the responder is on the way and gives a ten-minute estimate. A claim alone never promises travel, and an estimate is not a guarantee.
  8. History and accountability. History keeps the report and human decisions together. When new information arrives, the team can look back, reassess the situation, and update the next step.
  9. Team coverage. Coverage shows which approved teams serve each area, what help they can offer, and whether they have room for another request.
  10. Prepared training report. The training lab lets teams practice with a prepared fictional report. Process it once, then review the new case. That’s Pharos: a clearer handoff from a caller to the people who can help.
