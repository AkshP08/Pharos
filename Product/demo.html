What this demo shows

This is a fictional-data screen walkthrough in an isolated local Pharos runtime. It uses actual Beacon screens and backend transitions. Intake events and contextual review outputs are controlled fixtures; no real phone call, speech recognition, model inference, caller recording, or dispatch is demonstrated.

The walkthrough covers urgency ranking, synthetic transcript processing, conservative linked updates and case history, evidence-bound human review, reasoned acceptance, eligible responder assignment, and explicit desk-reported ETA. Production caller records, credentials, and provider dashboards are absent.

Human acceptance is shown as a fictional reviewer action. It does not prove the emergency is real. Reported danger stays visible during verification, and a claim alone never confirms travel or arrival.

Readable walkthrough

  1. See who needs attention. Reported needs set the suggested order. Service-area filters help responders focus.
  2. Turn words into a case. Process a sample transcript through the backend. Three people. A specific landmark. A shelter need.
  3. Keep the facts visible. Beacon preserves the caller’s words and location, then explains suggested urgency and coverage.
  4. Start with the current need. A fictional caller reports three people safely waiting for shelter near Riverbend footbridge.
  5. Let new danger change priority. The same fictional situation now includes rising indoor water and no safe exit. The continuing case is reassessed.
  6. Preserve how the situation changed. Two linked reports remain available. The latest facts guide the current response without erasing the earlier report.
  7. Hold response for human review. A fictional unsafe request has exact quoted evidence. Normal assignment and ETA are held; verification ownership remains possible.
  8. Make the decision accountable. The reviewer records a reason against the current evidence before accepting the report for response.
  9. Acceptance opens the next step. The decision is recorded with its evidence scope. Coverage and responder eligibility still require confirmation.
  10. Claiming establishes ownership. An approved fictional team claims the accepted case. A claim alone does not confirm travel or arrival.
  11. Report an estimate explicitly. Choose On the way and enter a fresh ten-minute estimate. The desk owns the status update.
  12. Be precise about reassurance. Beacon shows who reported the update and when. Arrival remains an estimate; time passing never confirms arrival.
