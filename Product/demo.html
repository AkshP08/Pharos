What was performed

The video records real navigation and actions in the deployed app: call-to-case navigation, a prepared fictional transcript submission, inspection of quoted concerns, a reasoned human acceptance, eligible responder assignment, and saving an explicit desk-reported estimate. Forms are prepared before their filmed action to keep the walkthrough brief.

The phone excerpt is real. A consenting test caller called the existing Photon hotline with fictional Northbank shelter details. The app recorded the completed session and generated its transcript. Only a short excerpt was captured after acknowledged audio consent, with explicit permission to publish it. The rest of the call is omitted; the video cuts forward to the completed transcript.

Training, review, assignment and ETA actions use a separate clearly fictional report. They do not dispatch real help. Unrelated private reports are obscured in the recording. No credentials, private phone numbers, provider dashboards, or unrelated call audio are published.

Reported danger remains visible during review. Human acceptance is not proof of truth; assignment alone is not travel or arrival; an ETA remains an estimate.

Narration transcript

  1. Response desk. Pharos turns a phone report into a shared response workflow. Beacon keeps reported urgency, service area, and human verification visible in one responder workspace.
  2. Real phone-call excerpt. First, a real call with fictional scenario details. [A short real call excerpt follows. Pharos asks for the situation and location; the caller reports Northbank Library South entrance, Zone 2.]
  3. Completed call and transcript. After the call, Beacon shows its completed session and generated speaker transcript. Responders can inspect the caller's exact words and open the linked case.
  4. Linked case and history. The case preserves location, needs, and priority explanations. New reports can update urgency while retaining earlier history. Suggested ranking supports human decisions; it does not dispatch help.
  5. Prepared training submission. The training lab accepts a prepared fictional transcript. One action processes it into a persistent case, making the intake workflow easy to rehearse without a phone call.
  6. Quoted evidence and review hold. Content concerns appear beside reported danger. Normal assignment and arrival estimates stay on hold until a human reviews the current evidence. Concern signals are not proof of deception.
  7. Human acceptance and assignment. Here, the reviewer records a reason and accepts the fictional report. An eligible responder can then claim ownership. Decisions remain tied to the evidence version and audit history.
  8. Explicit desk ETA. The desk explicitly reports On the way and enters a fresh estimate. Claiming alone never confirms travel, and elapsed time never confirms arrival. Updated facts can require reassessment.
  9. Responder coverage. Coverage profiles show service areas, capabilities, availability, and capacity. New profiles require coordinator approval before they can receive cases.
  10. Manual, tests and design. The product includes a user manual, test specification, and system design. Human responders own the final decisions.
